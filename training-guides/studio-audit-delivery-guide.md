# Studio Audit & Scope Framework — Delivery Guide

_Updated 24 September 2026_

_How to run any of the six Studio Audits/Assessments and produce the outputs the scope sheets promise. One process, six studios._

---

## Why one guide works for all six

Every scope sheet promises the same shape of output, with studio-specific labels:

| Universal output | AI | Strategy | Digital | Brand | Conversion | Insights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ranked opportunities (3–5)** | Opportunity map | Ranked growth blockers | Channel fix list | _(folded into consistency audit)_ | Experiment queue | _(folded into tracking gap report)_ |
| **Baseline / gap read** | Hours baseline | Positioning gap analysis | Waste report | Consistency audit | Drop-off map | Tracking gap report |
| **Structural / design output** | Review-gate design | Allocation gap analysis | Lifecycle flow map | Template gap list | Message-match matrix | Metric dictionary |
| **90-day roadmap** | ✓ | ✓ (as 90-day plan) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

If you can produce those four outputs credibly for a studio, the audit is deliverable. This guide gets you there in the four stages every scope sheet commits to: **Kickoff → Review → Findings drafted → Read-out.**

> **Not to be confused with the automated audits.** The **Website Audit**, **SEO Audit** and **AI Search Visibility Audit** are run from client-portal Admin and have their own guides. AI **Studio** is about how a team uses AI in its workflows. The AI **Search Visibility Audit** measures whether AI assistants name the client.

---

## Stage 1 — Kickoff (Day 1)

**1.1 Confirm studio fit.**
Use the "Which audit is which" framing from the overview sheet. Match the client's stated pain to the one-line descriptor; for example, "production work is eating your team's week" points to AI Studio. If it spans two studios, say so and pick the entry point using the overview sheet's rule.

**1.2 Send the intake.**
All six studios use the **v3 intake**. Send the client the Studio intake link (Audit app → **New Intake ▾ → Start a Studio intake**, or the `/intake` link). The flow is:

1. **Client name.** Use the name exactly as it appears on the Audit app's Clients page. Intakes match clients by exact name, and a different spelling creates a duplicate client.
2. **Client onboarding.** Asked once per client, the first time they fill in any Studio intake:
    - Who has final sign-off on recommendations, and who's the day-to-day point of contact?
    - Is there a deadline or event (launch, budget cycle, board meeting) driving the timeline?
    - Any tools, platforms, or vendors that are off-limits or locked in by contract?
    - Any data privacy, compliance, or brand guidelines we need to work within?
3. **Studio selection**, then the **studio's own questions**:

| Studio | Questions | Access checkboxes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI Studio | 11 | 5 |
| Strategy Studio | 10 | 2 |
| Digital Studio | 13 | 4 |
| Creative (Brand) Studio | 11 | 3 |
| Conversion Studio | 13 | 3 |
| Insights Studio | 14 | 4 |

A direct link to a single studio's intake still works. If the client hasn't done onboarding yet, it sends them through onboarding first and then back.

**1.3 Confirm access.**
The access list is part of each studio's intake. It appears as checkboxes under **"Please check everything you're able to grant us read access to:"**, and each answer shows as **Yes/No** on the report. Follow up before Day 2 on anything left **No**: read-only logins, asset access, and uploads to the client portal's **Research** category. Delays here eat directly into the 1–2 week timeline.

**1.4 Set the time commitment correctly.**
Match the studio's actual "Your Time" figure. Most are "2 calls, ~3 hrs", but Strategy is the outlier at **4–6 stakeholder interviews**. Flag this explicitly at kickoff. That scope sheet's "What we need from you" list names only one call, so don't let the client be surprised later.

## Stage 2 — Review (Days 2–7)

**2.1 Read the intake and onboarding answers.**
Read every answer before you open any accounts. The Audit app's **Review Intakes** page shows the studio answers, the access checklist as Yes/No, and the client's onboarding block. The intake answers should already point you to how the client sees their own problem. The onboarding answers set the constraints: who signs off, the deadline, locked-in vendors, and compliance.

**2.2 Run the studio's "What we review" checklist against live access.**
This is the evidence-gathering half of the audit, and it's largely independent of what the client said in intake. Work top to bottom through the studio's review areas. For Digital, for example: paid media accounts → email and lifecycle → organic and search → measurement hygiene.

**2.3 Cross-reference intake answers against what the accounts actually show.**
This is where raw access turns into insight. Where the client's stated belief and the account data disagree, that disagreement is usually the strongest candidate for a top-ranked opportunity.

**2.4 Log research documents as you go.**
Anything reusable beyond this engagement, such as competitor findings or category research, goes into the client's **Research** category in the portal, not just this audit's working files. Research builds up across the whole client relationship. The automated audits (Website, SEO, AI Search Visibility) save their evidence there too, so check it for existing material first.

---

## Stage 3 — Findings Drafted (Days 8–10)

**3.1 Draft Findings and an Executive Summary in Brandaify's voice.**
Write in Brandaify's own brand voice, not the client's, since clients won't reliably have a Brand Guide on file to draft against. The report generator already includes the client's onboarding answers, so drafts reflect the sign-off contact, deadlines and constraints.

**3.2 Rank 3–5 opportunities.**
For each one, state plainly its estimated impact, its estimated effort, and the specific number it should move. Put the most important one first, whether that's the worst problem or the highest return.

**3.3 Build the studio-specific baseline/gap output.**
This is where studio expertise matters most, and where missing intake answers hurt most:

- **AI** — the hours baseline per workflow needs numbers for each workflow. If the intake answers don't give comparable figures for each workflow, get them on the kickoff call.
- **Strategy** — the positioning gap analysis depends on the stakeholder interviews giving consistent, comparable answers. Without 4–6 of them, internal alignment can't really be assessed.
- **Insights** — the metric dictionary needs to know who uses which number for what, not just tooling access.
- **Brand** — Creative's intake captures refresh appetite, non-negotiables and admired references, which map directly to the refresh-or-overhaul decision.

**3.4 Build the 90-day roadmap.**
Sequence the approved opportunities, and name owners where the client has named people (e.g. Strategy's "named owners and first three moves"). Use the onboarding answers for the deadline and the sign-off contact. Keep the roadmap something the client can execute even if they never hire the Sprint.

**3.5 Run a traceability check before the read-out.**
Confirm that each opportunity and every deliverable traces back to either:

- something the client stated in the intake or onboarding, or
- something found in the account-level review.

Anything that traces to neither is speculation. Cut it, or go and get the missing input.

---

## Stage 4 — Read-out (End of Week 2)

**4.1 Walk the client through the findings** rather than just handing over a document.

**4.2 State plainly what you'd do first.** The scope sheets promise this explicitly, so don't leave it implicit in a prioritised list.

**4.3 Record which opportunities the client approves to move forward on.**
This is the handoff into the Sprint Agreement. Record each approved opportunity with its audit section reference, mirroring the Approved Audit Findings & Opportunities table in the Sprint Agreement template, so the two documents stay cross-referenced. In the Audit app, tick **"Approved for client"** on those opportunities, **Approve** the report, and share its `/client-view/[id]` link.

**4.4 Close the loop.**
The client has **no** commitment after the audit: they can hire the Sprint or run the roadmap in-house. Mean it; don't let it turn into a sales pitch.

---

## Pre-read-out quality check

Before any findings go to a client, confirm:

- [ ] Every ranked opportunity has impact, effort and a specific number attached
- [ ] Every deliverable traces to an intake or onboarding answer, or to a reviewed account or asset. Nothing is inferred without a source.
- [ ] Anything marked **No** on the access checklist was either resolved or is named in the report as a limit on what was reviewed
- [ ] Out-of-scope adjacent work is named explicitly, using each scope sheet's "Not in this audit" list, so the client knows what a Sprint would and wouldn't include
- [ ] The 90-day roadmap is sequenced and ownable without Brandaify in the room

## Known gap: no real submissions yet

All six studios now have a built **v3 intake** (listed in 1.2). However, **none has a real client submission on record yet**. The earlier Creative submissions belonged to a client that has since been removed. Until a studio's first real intake comes through:

- Test the studio's form end to end with a clearly named test client before sending it to a real client, and delete the test client afterwards.
- Treat the kickoff call as the backup for anything the form doesn't capture well. This matters most for AI Studio's hours per workflow and Strategy's multi-interview alignment check, the two outputs hardest to produce from a general conversation alone.
